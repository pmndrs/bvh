# Handoff: pmndrs/bvh after the split (2026-11-10)

This repo was split out of `DennisSmolek/webgpu-ddgi`, where the code lived in `packages/bvh`. The full plan is [docs/internals/design/bvh-package-split.md](docs/internals/design/bvh-package-split.md): the decisions (D1–D8), the target layout, phases P0–P9, examples, docs and tasks BX.0–BX.20. Read it first.

## Rules the work runs under (from Dennis)

- **The core and three matter most.** Other tools of Dennis's need this BVH, and they shouldn't have to pull it out of the GI repo.
- **Engine-free core**, with three and Babylon behind subpaths. Babylon is "it works" only (D5). WebGL2 isn't a target.
- **Feature-forward WebGPU:**
  - `subgroups` first, with one declared fallback (tinybvh on the CPU);
  - no parallel GPU paths.
- **Faithful port first.** Ports of tinybvh's H-PLOC and HIPRT's LBVH stay as published. Any speed change is a measured lever, with a note on why and how to undo it.
- **Licensed code only.** Nothing is ported from repos without a licence (D8).
- **Hardware:**
  - RTX 3060 and M5 Pro are the targets;
  - the Intel UHD is for correctness only;
  - benches run on a loaded GPU;
  - no build may take minutes.
- **Correctness gates, not timing gates**, at this prototype stage.
- **Models:** Opus for code and review, Sonnet or Haiku for small tasks, docs and tests.
- **Git:** commit locally; pushing `main` is fine; no npm publish, tags or releases.
- **Levers:** a flexible setting with a measured win may become the default (the 256 MiB cap).
- **The package ends at the hit** (2026-11-13). The library covers building, refitting, scene trees, the walk and spatial queries. A hit carries the triangle, instance, barycentrics, face normal and an opaque material number.
  - Anything that decides what a hit *looks like* goes in Dennis's separate path tracer repo, or in the shared tracing layer above us: sampling, random numbers, BSDFs, lights, accumulation, textures, and shading-normal or UV interpolation.
  - Physics-style queries (shapecast, closest point, boxes) stay here.
  - Check every consumer request against this rule. Per-triangle materials passed because the material is only a number.
  - `examples/three/pathtracer` is a demo of the walk. It and its pending compare page may move to the new repo.

  - `mergeStaticInstances` is **on** in `SceneTrees` and `buildTraceScene`. It only merges instances marked `static: true` (added before the first update, in `SceneTrees`); a scene that marks nothing is unchanged. - **API:** `traceModuleWgsl({ queries: true })` adds `bvhClosestPoint(point, maxDistance) -> BvhClosestHit` (distance, instance, triangle, material, barycentrics, point, normal), `bvhIntersectsSphere`, `bvhIntersectsBox`, `bvhIntersectsOrientedBox`, each with `*Masked` unless `rayMasks: false`; `{ shapecast: wgsl }` compiles the caller's hooks (`bvhShapecastBounds` score, `bvhShapecastKeep` on pop, `bvhShapecastTriangle` with world corners) into `bvhShapecast()`. `ClosestPointBatchKernel` / `closestPointKernelWgsl` (16-byte queries in, 48-byte `CLOSEST_POINT_RECORD_WORD` records out, one mask per batch, refuses other formats). Helpers `bvhIntersectRayTriangle` (the walk's watertight test, not a copy) and `bvhClosestPointToTriangle` in every module, binding-free in `triangleGeometryWgsl()`. `/three`: `BvhTraceNodes` options `queries` and `shapecast`, TSL `closestPoint` / `closestPointMasked` (`bvhClosestPointHitStruct`).
- **How:** one shapecast walk template stamped per hook set (the ray walk's shape plus a score stack); queries stay in world space and each instance is inverted once on entry (mesh boxes become padded world boxes, corners are moved), so scaled, rotated and mirrored instances give world distances. Every node and triangle format and merged scenes work through the walk's own loaders. Off by default: without the options the module is the ray walk alone.
- **Checked:** `tests/gpu/pages/query-oracle` (`profile=ci` in the SwiftShader job) against `CpuMeshBvh` per instance in world space: M5 full profile 0 mismatches over 9 variants (f32/quantized, records/indexed/packed, merged, no masks, triangle materials); `three-trace` checks TSL `closestPoint` bit for bit against the batch kernel. `trace-oracle`, `trace-inline` pass. Ray walk unchanged (every declaration the same text); Sponza surface bench `profile=quick`, three alternating pairs: new/old median 1.007.
- **Review fixes (2026-11-13):** a caller's `bvhShapecastTriangle` may now call other walks (the shapecast re-reads its quantized frame after each leaf; `bvhTrianglePasses` and the bounds/keep hooks must not, documented, ray walk unchanged); box padding scales with the transform's terms, not the result (a far-from-origin instance let corners out); `bvhClosestPointToTriangle` measures degenerate triangles and slivers (thinner than about 1:3000) to their edges instead of NaN or a wrong region; `TraceBatchKernel` and `ClosestPointBatchKernel` refuse buffers too small for `count`. `query-oracle` gained the far scene, degenerate triangles, a mask-0 instance, `maxDistance` Infinity/0/-1, a nested `bvhTraceAny` check against the CPU walk and a corner-padding check; M5 full profile 0 mismatches. SwiftShader `profile=ci` locally: `query-oracle` 43 s next to `trace-oracle` 84 s, `trace-inline` 15 s (the CI job's four pages about 2.4 min before the runner's slowdown).
- **CPU finding (for the `src/query` owner):** `CpuMeshBvh.closestPointToPoint` (three's region algorithm, in doubles) picks a wrong region on slivers degenerate only up to rounding (a collinear triangle under a general transform): in the oracle's first try it gave 2.2e-3 for a point lying on such a sliver (the GPU: 1.2e-7) and a distance 0.3% too long in another case. The GPU treats slivers as edges; the oracle keeps its degenerate triangles exact to stay comparable.
