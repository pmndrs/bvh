# Divergences: where our code differs from its sources, and why

Dennis's rule: ports stay faithful to their source. Any change is a measured lever, with a note on why and how to undo it. This file is the one list of every change.

How to read it:

- Each row is one difference. Columns: what differs, what the source does, what we do, why (with the measurement, if one is written down), how to get the source's behaviour back, and where it lives.
- "Reason not recorded" means no doc or comment says why. Those rows are listed again under [Open](#open).
- "Not switchable" means the source's behaviour is not available. Getting it back means changing code.
- Paths without a prefix are in this repo. **ddgi** means `~/Developer/webgpu-ddgi`, the repo this code was split out of. Its docs are cited by file and entry, not by line, because they keep moving: `NOTES` is `docs/NOTES.md`, `DECISIONS` is `docs/DECISIONS.md`, `PROGRESS` is `PROGRESS.md`.
- Numbers are as written in those docs. Hardware is the RTX 3060 Laptop unless a row says otherwise. Scenes are Sponza (5.74 M triangles, 132 meshes) unless said.
- Checked: this file was written from the docs and the code comments in this repo and in ddgi. No source repo (tinybvh, HIPRT) was available locally, so "source does" cells repeat what our comments and notes say about the source. Nothing was checked against the source's code.

## 1. tinybvh

### 1a. The WebAssembly build and wrapper

tinybvh's headers are compiled unchanged. The differences are in how we compile and wrap them.

### 1b. `BuildHPLOC`, ported to WGSL and to a CPU twin

Ported step for step from `BVH::BuildHPLOC` (`tiny_bvh_base.h`) into `src/gpu-hploc-wgsl.ts` (GPU) and `src/hploc-cpu.ts` (CPU twin). Search radius 8 and merge threshold 16 are kept as in tinybvh.

With `mortonCodes: 'tinybvh-63'`, `tieBreak: 'tinybvh'` and `maxLeafPrimitives: 1`, the CPU twin builds exactly tinybvh's tree. A unit test checks this against tinybvh itself (`buildLayout(geometry, { builder: 'hploc', maxLeafPrimitives: 1 })`, `src/hploc-cpu.test.ts:54-55`). The GPU's SAH cost is then within 1% of tinybvh's on the `bvh-hploc` page.

| # | What differs | Source does | We do | Why | Get the source's behaviour back | Where |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | Morton codes | 63-bit codes, 21 bits per axis, each axis stretched over the mesh's box (two sort words) | Default `'cubic-30'`: 30 bits in cubic cells over the mesh's centroid box (one sort word) | Sponza SAH cost against tinybvh's default build: 1.034x with cubic codes, 1.078x with tinybvh's codes (CPU twins, NOTES "H-PLOC trees are close to tinybvh's best build"). One sort word instead of two. The cubic cells are the same fix as L1 | `mortonCodes: 'tinybvh-63'` | `src/hploc-cpu.ts:66-79`; `src/gpu-hploc-wgsl.ts:273-280`; `src/gpu-hploc.ts:89` |
| P2 | Exact area ties | Picks the first of equally good neighbours, in the order +1, -1, +2, -2... On exact ties every cluster picks its right neighbour and the tree becomes a chain | Default `'pairs'`: an exact tie goes to the nearer neighbour, then the pair that starts on an even place, then the lower place. Ties pair up as (0,1), (2,3)... | 3000 identical triangles give depth 128 with tinybvh's rule, twice the tracer's 64-entry stack. Pairs give depth 9. It also made a 1.8% cheaper tree on a test grid (NOTES, "tinybvh's H-PLOC chains the tree on exact area ties") | `tieBreak: 'tinybvh'`. (The `bvh-hploc` page refuses the 3000-identical-triangles mesh in that mode, since tinybvh's tree is 128 deep) | `src/hploc-cpu.ts:15-25,86,218`; `src/gpu-hploc-wgsl.ts:521-529` |
| P3 | SAH leaf collapse and leaf size | Leaves of one triangle | After the climb, a subtree that is cheaper as one leaf becomes one (at most `maxLeafPrimitives`, default 8; costs 1 and 1). Decided as each node is made, then two scans number the kept nodes and place the leaves' triangles | Sponza SAH cost against tinybvh's default build: 1.034x with collapse, against 1.172x for tinybvh's own one-triangle-leaf `BuildHPLOC` (NOTES, "H-PLOC trees are close to tinybvh's best build"). This is B1's rule, reused | `maxLeafPrimitives: 1` (no inner node can then be cheaper as a leaf). The CPU twin's note says tinybvh's trees result with this option and its codes and tie rule | `src/hploc-cpu.ts:39-45,297-340`; `src/gpu-hploc-wgsl.ts:91-97,534-554`; `src/gpu-hploc.ts:25-28`; `src/mesh-input.ts` (`GpuBuildOptions`) |
| P4 | The merge runs in a subgroup, with cluster slots | Serial CPU code (the paper and NexusBVH use one cluster per lane of a 32-wide warp, see H1) | The whole subgroup merges one node at a time. Cluster `c` sits in lane `c % size`, slot `c / size`; `ceil(32 / smallest subgroup size)` slots per lane. Shuffles go by cluster number | Intel UHD runs 8 or 16 wide, so one cluster per lane does not fit. The tree is the same on every subgroup size: the RTX and the Intel build identical trees (PROGRESS B2). One slot on 32-wide GPUs, four on the Intel | Not switchable. (On a 32-wide GPU it already is the one-slot case.) If the browser does not report `subgroupMinSize`, 4 is assumed (8 slots per lane): correct, slower | `src/gpu-hploc-wgsl.ts:46-62`; `src/gpu-hploc.ts:85,167-172` |
| P5 | Words handed between threads | tinybvh's `BuildHPLOC` is scalar CPU code, so nothing is handed over | Every word one climbing thread hands to another is a 32-bit atomic, written once after a clear pass, read with a bounded wait. Stored cluster entries carry their writer's radix-tree level, because positions are reused. A wait that runs out refuses the tree | WGSL has no fence across workgroups (see L3). Stale-write case is documented in the WGSL; it would refuse a tree, not corrupt it (PROGRESS B2 review) | Not switchable | `src/gpu-hploc-wgsl.ts:64-80` |
| P6 | Node numbering | Reason not recorded what tinybvh does here (our comment says counting nodes with one atomic per mesh and merge round was the alternative) | Nodes are numbered without a counter. One number stays unused and the layout skips it | Our comment: a per-mesh atomic counter queued hundreds of thousands of atomics on one word for Sponza's big meshes. NOTES (B2) measured only about 5% from removing it, "but numbering nodes without it is simpler anyway" | Not switchable | `src/gpu-hploc-wgsl.ts:82-90`; NOTES, "One atomic counter per mesh" |
| P7 | Limits | No limit recorded in our docs | A mesh may have at most `2^24 - 1` triangles. A tree with a radix-tree level above 126 is refused. | P8 | GPU rounding | Float on the CPU | The GPU's division (WGSL allows 2.5 ulp) and any fused multiply-add can round differently. The CPU twin rounds to f32 at every step, which matches except where the GPU differs | Not a choice. ## 2. HIPRT's LBVH

MIT, Advanced Micro Devices. Pinned at commit `0629d08568fff39f9a36bf9ee800fc7bac5bb096` (`THIRD_PARTY.md`). Ported: `MortonCode.h`, and from `LbvhBuilderKernels.h` the key-plus-index compare (`findHighestDifferentBit`), `findParent` and the climb of `EmitTopologyAndFitBounds` (Apetrei 2014). The file's own list of differences is at the top of `src/gpu-lbvh-wgsl.ts:37-69`. The CPU twin is `src/lbvh-cpu.ts` (tests and cross-checks, not a builder to ship).

| # | What differs | Source does | We do | Why | Get the source's behaviour back | Where |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| L1 | Morton cells | `computeMortonCode` stretches each axis to 1024 cells | Cubic cells over each mesh's centroid box: 1024 along the longest side, the same cell size on the other axes | Stretched cells split a flat or long mesh across its thin side as often as along its long sides. SAH cost 2.4-2.6 times tinybvh's on slabs, rods and flat grids, against 1.02-1.22 with cubic cells. Cubes are unchanged. Real meshes are often walls, floors and curtains (NOTES "Morton codes need cubic cells"; B1 CPU twin) | Not switchable in `GpuLbvhBuilder`. (H-PLOC has `'tinybvh-63'`, but that is tinybvh's 63-bit stretched code, not HIPRT's 30-bit one) | `src/gpu-lbvh-wgsl.ts:548-554`; `src/lbvh-cpu.ts:71` |
| L2 | 64-bit compare | Compares key and index as 64-bit integers | Compares a pair of `u32`, high word first | WGSL has no 64-bit integers. Same result | Not applicable | `src/gpu-lbvh-wgsl.ts:39-40,658` |
| L3 | Memory visibility | Relies on `__threadfence()` so the first child's plain stores are visible to the second | Everything one climbing thread hands to another goes through 32-bit atomic stores and loads (bit copies only). The reader waits, bounded (4096 re-reads), for the clear pass's "unwritten" mark to go. A wait that runs out is counted and the builder refuses the tree | WGSL has no fence across workgroups. A one-triangle leaf is never stored: the second thread reads its box itself | Halves the handed-over words. On Sponza the climb's time hardly moved (13 ms either way): it is not bound by how many words are handed over (NOTES, "The LBVH climb is not bound by how many words it hands over") | Not switchable | `src/gpu-lbvh-wgsl.ts:48-56` |

| L7 | Batches | A separate batch builder | One builder: every mesh of a batch shares each dispatch. | L8 | Two-round climb | One climb | The first round stops a thread when its node has 64 triangles (`LBVH_FIRST_ROUND_TRIANGLES`) and queues the arrival for the second round, an indirect dispatch | In a subgroup one lane keeps climbing after the others stopped, so the subgroup stays resident. ## 3. Woop, Benthin and Wald: watertight ray/triangle test

"Watertight Ray/Triangle Intersection", 2013. In `src/trace/wgsl.ts` (GPU) and `src/trace/cpu-traversal.ts` (CPU port). The tracer has this test only. It stores each triangle's three corners as they are, so a corner shared by two triangles has identical bits in both.

| # | What differs | Source does | We do | Why | Get the source's behaviour back | Where |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Edge functions in a fixed corner order (the Metal fix) | The plain formulation: each edge function is `x1*y1 - x2*y2`, rounded by the compiler however it likes | `bvhEdgeFunction` orders an edge's two corners (by x, then y), so both triangles sharing an edge run the same arithmetic on the same operands. The triangle that walks the edge the other way negates the result, which is exact. An edge that shears to one point is set to exactly 0 | Dawn compiles Metal in relaxed math mode, which lets the compiler fuse one product into the subtraction (an FMA). Two triangles then did not get exact negatives, and on an Apple M5 Pro 2,696 of 1M shared-edge rays (1,809 transformed) slipped through; 0 after the fix. NVIDIA and Intel were not affected. About 0.1% of rays. Cost: about 3 compares and 4 selects per edge, three edges per test; the bench was too noisy to show a cost (ddgi commit 0d1c91a; ddgi NOTES "The watertight test needs its edge functions in a fixed corner order"). It was committed in ddgi after the code was moved, and ported here in `7d62375` | Not switchable. It is one path for every backend. Revert the commit (ddgi 0d1c91a) to get the plain edge functions. The CPU port is unchanged (doubles are never fused) | `src/trace/wgsl.ts` (`bvhEdgeFunction`); from ddgi commit 0d1c91a |
| T2 | Ray shear done with per-ray row vectors | A per-ray axis permutation (`kx`, `ky`, `kz`) used as indices into the corners | Three per-ray row vectors built once with `select()`; each corner is transformed by dot products. No runtime vector indexing. Same arithmetic: products with 0 and 1 are exact, so rounding is as before and shared corners still transform identically | Indexing a vector by a per-ray value (`a[kx]`) can push it into slow per-thread scratch memory. Sponza 61 to 101 Mrays/s on the RTX 3060 once removed; watertight cost 45% on NVIDIA against 20% on Intel before (`docs/internals/design/trace.md`, parts B and C) | Not switchable (the indexed version was replaced in T0.10 part C) | `src/trace/wgsl.ts:370-426`; `src/trace/cpu-traversal.ts:55-80` (the CPU port still names `kx, ky, kz`) |
| T3 | An edge function of exactly 0 | Reason not recorded what the paper does here | A zero counts as inside for both triangles, so a ray exactly on an edge hits both neighbours rather than neither | Stated as the design in the comment; no measurement recorded | Not switchable | `src/trace/wgsl.ts:472-476,497-499`; `src/trace/cpu-traversal.ts:261` |
| T4 | CPU port in doubles | f32 | The CPU port of the walk runs in doubles, so it matches the GPU's decisions but not its rounding | It exists to test the builder and the walk logic in Node, with no GPU. It also reports the deepest stack, which the GPU cannot | Not switchable | `src/trace/cpu-traversal.ts:1-9` |

## 4. H-PLOC, the paper, and NexusBVH

Benthin et al., HPG 2024, "H-PLOC: Hierarchical Parallel Locally-Ordered Clustering for Bounding Volume Hierarchy Construction". Our code follows tinybvh's `BuildHPLOC` (section 1b), which tinybvh says builds the same trees as NexusBVH. NexusBVH (Apache-2.0, CUDA) "was read about, not copied" (`THIRD_PARTY.md`). So the rows below are differences from the paper's and NexusBVH's design only. Everything else about the climb is in section 1b. The bottom-up climb itself is Apetrei's (rows L3 to L8).

| # | What differs | Source does | We do | Why | Get the source's behaviour back | Where |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| H1 | One cluster per lane | One cluster per lane of a 32-wide warp | Slots per lane (row P4) | Subgroup sizes of 4 to 128 exist; Intel UHD is 8 or 16 | On a 32-wide GPU the code is the one-slot case | `src/gpu-hploc-wgsl.ts:46-57` |
| H2 | Morton code width | 64-bit codes | Default 30-bit (one sort word). 63-bit available | WGSL has no `u64`, so 64-bit codes are emulated as pairs of `u32`, which costs twice the radix-sort passes (8 instead of 4) (`docs/internals/design/bvh-framework.md`, "Code we port", item 3). See P1 for the quality measurement | `mortonCodes: 'tinybvh-63'` (63 bits, not 64) | `src/hploc-cpu.ts:66-79` |

## 5. Radix sort, scans, refit and instance boxes

`THIRD_PARTY.md` lists these as "ours". They follow published practice and have no single source, so there is no faithful-port rule to break. These rows are listed so the choices are in one place.

| # | What differs | Source does | We do | Why | Get the source's behaviour back | Where |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| S1 | No decoupled look-back in the sort and scan | One-sweep radix sort with decoupled look-back (Gridwise, Apache-2.0, was the candidate to vendor, "minus the look-back default"). HIPRT uses Orochi's radix sort | Reduce-then-scan, stable and segmented, 5-bit digits (6 passes for 30 bits). Three dispatches for a scan: reduce, scan across tiles, downsweep. The dispatch boundaries do the synchronising | WebGPU does not promise forward progress between workgroups, so a look-back could hang. "Gridwise's own docs say look-back can fail on Apple and ARM" (`docs/internals/design/bvh-framework.md`, "What changes from the T0.10 design", Sort and scan row). The framework allowed "one-sweep decoupled look-back only as an optional fast path behind a self-test". It was not built. The speed cost against a look-back sort: not recorded (the sort took 5.8 ms in the last LBVH profile and 8.7 ms in the H-PLOC profile, single builds at a low clock: PROGRESS B1, B2) | Not switchable. Would need a new sort | `src/gpu-radix-sort.ts:1-8,35`; `src/gpu-scan.ts:1-16` |
| S2 | Thread order in the workgroup scan | `local_invocation_index` | Each subgroup's elected lane claims a block of thread numbers from a workgroup counter; lanes take their rank in it. Thread numbers then run in subgroup lane order, which is what `subgroupExclusiveAdd` sums in | WebGPU fixes neither the subgroup size (4 to 128; Intel's UHD runs 8 or 16 wide, and it may differ between pipelines on one device) nor the lane layout | Not switchable | `src/gpu-workgroup-scan.ts:1-20` |
| S3 | Refit: no float atomics, bounded wait, repair pass | The framework names "standard practice in production engines" and no single source | One thread per leaf climbs; the second to arrive at a node (per-node counter) takes the union. Boxes are 32-bit atomic words. A bounded re-read covers WGSL's relaxed order. If a read gives up, a one-workgroup repair pass refits the whole tree level by level, so a wrong box is never kept | Same reasons as L3. WGSL has no float atomics | Not switchable | `src/gpu-refit.ts:1-40`; `docs/internals/design/bvh-framework.md` ("What changes from the T0.10 design", Deforming meshes row) |
| S4 | Instance world box: padding on Arvo's method | Arvo's method (Graphics Gems, 1990) gives exactly the box around the eight transformed corners | Same method, with each axis grown by `BVH_INSTANCE_BOX_PADDING` = 2^-20 times the summed magnitude of every term | In f32, each sum can be off by a few ulp of its largest term, and with cancellation (a large translation, say) the error can exceed the result. 2^-20 is sixteen f32 steps. A loose instance box only decides which instances a ray enters, so it costs nothing measurable (the comment's claim; no number recorded) | Set `BVH_INSTANCE_BOX_PADDING` to 0 (a constant; no option) | `src/gpu-instance-box.ts:7-24` |

## Intentional design choices that aren't divergences

These look like differences but have no source to differ from, or they match it.

- **The tracer's walk is ours, not a port.** One loop with one 64-entry stack for both levels. Closest-hit visits the nearer child first; any-hit visits the **farther** child first by default (1.1-2.8x faster on the bench scenes with the same answers; T1.3), or the larger child with `anyHitOrder: 'larger'` (for occlusion rays from outside or across open space; trace.md, "Any-hit child order"). On the RTX 3060 (2026-11-13), `'larger'` was 1.11-1.48x far-first on instanced trees and 0.80-0.87x on merged surface rays, the same pattern as the M5 (`WINDOWS-RUNBOOK.md`, section 7). Back-face hits count (DDGI uses them). Rays in object space are not renormalized, so `t` stays in world units. Front face is the authored side, also under mirrored transforms. See `docs/internals/design/trace.md` and `src/trace/wgsl.ts:152-160`. The plan said to cross-check the walk against tinybvh's `traverse_bvh2.cl`; whether that was done is not recorded (see Open).
- **The node layout** (64 bytes, two child boxes, Aila-Laine) is tinybvh's `BVH_GPU` layout, "byte for byte ours" (`docs/internals/design/bvh-framework.md`). The triangle record is ours (W4).
- **Kept as published:** H-PLOC's merge threshold 16 and search radius 8 (tinybvh and NexusBVH use 8; 16 is "half the 32-wide wave of the paper", `src/hploc-cpu.ts:62-66`); Apetrei's index tie-break in the LBVH (HIPRT's rule); the Arvo box formula (S4).
- **The WASM is tinybvh's headers, unchanged.** Only the flags and the wrapper differ (W1-W6). The licences travel inside the `.wasm` file's TypeScript wrapper (`THIRD_PARTY.md`).

## Open

Rows where a reason, a measurement or the source's behaviour is not written down anywhere I looked. Triaged 2026-11-13:
- **A GPU run** answers item 1.
- **A read of the pinned sources** (tinybvh, HIPRT, the paper) answers items 2, 3, 4, 7 and 9.
- **A bench** answers item 5, against a look-back sort.
- **A read of our own code** answers item 6, or one constant changes so the CPU and GPU pad the same.
- **Item 8** needs a pinned-emsdk machine.

No item needs a decision from Dennis, only the work.

None of them asks Dennis to choose anything. Once a read or run gives an answer, the row becomes a normal divergence row or is dropped.

1. **T1 needs GPU re-runs on NVIDIA and Intel** *(GPU run)*. The fix is ported (`7d62375`). On 2026-11-13 the RTX 3060 ran the surface-ray benches at `63af716`, which include the fix, and every paired answer agreed (`WINDOWS-RUNBOOK.md`, section 7). The `trace-oracle` page's shared-edge count on NVIDIA was not reported, and Intel has not run. On Metal it holds from this repo: on an Apple M5 (2026-11-11, branch `bx-walk-api`) the `trace-oracle` page let 0 of 2M shared-edge rays slip in each of its five variants.
2. **T3: what the paper does for an edge function of exactly 0.** *(source read)* Our comment says zero counts as inside for both triangles. No doc says whether the paper does the same, or whether we dropped a fallback (for example a double-precision retry). Not recorded.
3. **L5 and L6: what HIPRT's LBVH does** *(source read)* about leaf collapse and about subgroup or warp pairing. Our comments list the collapse as a difference but do not say what HIPRT does. Also the measured effect of each on the LBVH alone is not recorded. Whether `maxLeafPrimitives: 1` really gives no collapse in the LBVH has not been tested in any doc.
4. **P6: what tinybvh's `BuildHPLOC` does for node numbering.** *(source read)* Our comment names a per-mesh counter as the alternative. Also two notes disagree on the gain: the WGSL comment implies a large cost (hundreds of thousands of queued atomics), NOTES measured about 5%.
5. **S1: the speed cost of having no look-back.** *(bench)* The choice is justified by forward progress, but no comparison against a look-back sort is recorded.
6. **S4: why the CPU top level pads by 2^-21 of the result while the GPU pads by 2^-20 of the summed term magnitudes.** *(our code)* The comment says "for the same reason". The different sizes are not explained.
7. **Walk against tinybvh's `traverse_bvh2.cl`:** *(source read)* the framework planned a cross-check. No doc records that it was done or what it found.
8. **W1: the toolchain choice has no measurement of a scalar build in this repo.** *(pinned emsdk)* The 3x and 5.3 s to 2.2-2.7 s figures are from ddgi docs. A Linux or macOS `.wasm` rebuild has not been compared with the committed one (`docs/internals/design/bvh-builders.md`).
9. **Checked against nothing:** *(source read)* no source repo was available locally. If you want any "source does" cell verified against the source's code, it needs a read of tinybvh at the pinned commit and of HIPRT at its pinned commit.
