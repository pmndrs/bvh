The comparison suite in `compare/` runs the same geometry and the same seeded rays, boxes, spheres and points through
every library it covers. Each answer is checked against ours. **A library that disagrees with ours on a measurement
gets no ratio on it: a faster wrong answer is not faster.** The full tables, the method and every caveat are in
[`compare/RESULTS.md`](https://github.com/pmndrs/bvh/blob/main/compare/RESULTS.md), and the raw rows are in
`bench/results/compare.csv`.

> [!NOTE]
> Every number here is from one Apple M5 Pro (Node 22 and headless Chrome), and is **indicative**. The RTX 3060 runs are
> still to come.

## The libraries

- [three-mesh-bvh](https://github.com/gkjohnson/three-mesh-bvh) 0.9.18: `MeshBVH` (CENTER and SAH), `ObjectBVH`, and `three-mesh-bvh/webgpu`.
- [three](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js) 0.187: `Mesh.raycast`, the no-BVH baseline.
- [bvh-tree](https://github.com/benraziel/bvh-tree) 1.0.1: a plain-JS triangle BVH.
- [Babylon.js](https://github.com/BabylonJS/Babylon.js) 9.35: an engine's own picking, with and without the submesh octree.
- [bvh.js](https://github.com/agargaro/bvh.js) 0.0.13: a scene-level box BVH (objects, not triangles).
- [Rapier](https://github.com/dimforge/rapier.js) 0.21: a physics engine's ray, point and shape queries on a trimesh collider.

## Headline results

| What | Result (ours = 1; above 1 means faster than ours) |
| --- | --- |
| CPU closest-hit rays, Sponza (5.74M triangles) | ours 580k rays/s; three-mesh-bvh 0.75 (SAH) and 0.57 (CENTER), Rapier 0.95, bvh-tree 0.12 |
| Tree quality, Sponza (SAH cost, lower is better) | ours 90; three-mesh-bvh 122 (SAH) and 141 (CENTER); bvh-tree 205 |
| GPU rays, against three-mesh-bvh/webgpu (paired rounds) | ours 0.96–1.12× theirs closest-hit and 1.25–1.28× any-hit on Sponza and the knot; 0.85–0.91× closest on the furnished room. Any-hit from outside the room: 0.32× with the default order, 1.73× with `anyHitOrder: 'larger'` |
| Scene-level trees, 10k objects | ours is ahead of three-mesh-bvh's `ObjectBVH` on every query |

## Where other libraries win

Measured on the same harness. These are open items, not footnotes:

- **Rapier** answers box queries 2.3–2.7× faster and closest point 1.7–2.4× faster.
- **three-mesh-bvh, CPU:** refits 1.6–2× faster (it was 6–7× until our refit stopped sorting and allocating per node: ours went from 1,001 to 359 ms on Sponza; we still rewrite 48-byte records), and answers box queries 1.2–1.4× faster.
- **three-mesh-bvh, GPU:** its closest-hit walk is 1.1–1.2× ours on the furnished room. Its any-hit from outside the room was 3.2× our default kernel's: our any-hit enters the farther child first (right for shadow rays from surfaces), and a few rays per SIMD group of 32 went deep into the far side's furniture. With `anyHitOrder: 'larger'` (enter the larger child first) ours is 1.73× theirs on those rays; it stays a setting because it costs 0.78× on merged surface rays ([trace design](https://github.com/pmndrs/bvh/blob/main/docs/internals/design/trace.md)).
- **bvh.js** builds its scene tree about 3× faster.
- **Memory:** in the default layout we hold about 9–10× more bytes beyond the app's geometry than three-mesh-bvh, because our triangle records copy the corners. The [memory page](/performance/memory) shows the levers: indexed leaves and compressed nodes roughly halve it. Building one huge mesh with tinybvh grows its WebAssembly memory to about 230 bytes per triangle while the build runs (Sponza as one mesh: 1.3 GiB); the builder now drops that memory afterwards and the worker pool ends such a worker, so the build leaves 391 MiB behind instead of 1.7 GiB ([workers](/workers/workers)).
