The comparison suite in `compare/` runs the same geometry and the same seeded rays, boxes, spheres and points through
every library it covers. Each answer is checked against ours. **A library that disagrees with ours on a measurement
gets no ratio on it: a faster wrong answer is not faster.** The full tables, the method and every caveat are in
[`compare/RESULTS.md`](https://github.com/pmndrs/bvh/blob/main/compare/RESULTS.md), and the raw rows are in
`bench/results/compare.csv`.

> [!NOTE]
> Two machines, both **indicative**. The [2026-11-16 re-run](#re-run-on-the-windows-laptop-2026-11-16) used the
> defaults of 2026-11-15: the GPU on the RTX 3060 Laptop, the CPU on its Intel Core i9-11900H (Node 24). The rest of
> this page is the Apple M5 Pro (Node 22, headless Chrome) of 2026-11-12 and 2026-11-13, with `f32` GPU nodes (the
> default then). The M5's box, closest-point and refit numbers were measured while other projects loaded the machine
> (load average 8–10).

## The libraries

- [three-mesh-bvh](https://github.com/gkjohnson/three-mesh-bvh) 0.9.18: `MeshBVH` (CENTER and SAH), `ObjectBVH`, and `three-mesh-bvh/webgpu`.
- [three](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js) 0.187: `Mesh.raycast`, the no-BVH baseline.
- [bvh-tree](https://github.com/benraziel/bvh-tree) 1.0.1: a plain-JS triangle BVH.
- [Babylon.js](https://github.com/BabylonJS/Babylon.js) 9.35: an engine's own picking, with and without the submesh octree.
- [bvh.js](https://github.com/agargaro/bvh.js) 0.0.13: a scene-level box BVH (objects, not triangles).
- [Rapier](https://github.com/dimforge/rapier.js) 0.21: a physics engine's ray, point and shape queries on a trimesh collider.

## Re-run on the Windows laptop, 2026-11-16

What the 2026-11-15 defaults could change, measured again (the full tables are in
[`compare/RESULTS.md`](https://github.com/pmndrs/bvh/blob/main/compare/RESULTS.md)). Each row says which way its ratio goes.

| What | Result |
| --- | --- |
| GPU closest hits against three-mesh-bvh/webgpu, RTX 3060 | ours 1.29–1.33× theirs on the knot, 1.09–1.41× on Sponza, **0.51–0.86× on the furnished room** |
| GPU any hits | ours 1.18–1.43× on the knot and Sponza; the room 0.35× from outside (1.21× with `anyHitOrder: 'larger'`) and 0.87× from inside |
| CPU refit, Sponza | three-mesh-bvh 239 ms, ours 595 ms (441 ms with `corners: 'positions'`, what `/three` builds) |
| Memory beyond the geometry, Sponza | three-mesh-bvh 51.5 MiB; ours 459.7 MiB, 284.4 MiB with `corners: 'positions'`; on the GPU 355.5 against 214.3 MiB |
| Scene trees, 10k / 100k objects | bvh.js builds in 0.26× / 0.36× our time; `ObjectBVH` updates in 0.43× / 0.42× |

**Where others still win, on these machines:**

- **The furnished room on the GPU.** From outside, three-mesh-bvh traces about twice as many closest-hit rays per
  second as ours (ours 0.51× theirs; 0.96× with `f32` nodes). The cause is not the quantization grid. When a ray enters
  two boxes at exactly the same distance, our walk takes the left one first; from outside the room that is often the
  half behind the wall. Quantized boxes make such ties more common. Since 2026-11-17 the walk takes the other box on
  these ties: ours is 2.3× theirs on this set, with every other set level. From outside, its any hit is 2.8×
  our default order's rate, and 0.83× ours with `'larger'`.
- **CPU refit:** three-mesh-bvh is 2.2–2.5× faster than our records tree on every scene, and 1.6–1.9× faster than
  `corners: 'positions'`. Our tree has 3.6× the boxes (leaves of about 2 triangles against 7).
- **Memory:** on Sponza ours holds 5.5× three-mesh-bvh's bytes with `corners: 'positions'` and 8.9× with records.
- **CPU rays on this x86 laptop:** Rapier 1.10–1.25× our closest-hit rate on all three scenes, three-mesh-bvh with SAH
  1.14–1.47× on the knot and the room. On the M5 ours led (below). One run, not explained.
- **bvh.js** builds a scene tree about 3× faster; three-mesh-bvh's `ObjectBVH` updates 2.3× faster than our CPU top level.
- Ours still leads every GPU ray set on the knot and Sponza, box and closest-point queries (Rapier's closest point on the
  knot is a tie), and tree quality.

## Where other libraries win (Apple M5, 2026-11-13)

Measured on the same harness. These are open items, not footnotes; the 2026-11-16 list above is newer:

- **Rapier** ties our closest point on the torus knot (0.99–1.04×). Until the CPU query pass of 2026-11-13 it answered box queries 2.3–2.7× and closest point 1.7–2.4× faster than ours; ours now leads both everywhere else (Rapier 0.56–0.88× on boxes, 0.64–0.77× on closest point). Both queries became flat walks over the typed arrays with no objects per node or triangle, giving the same answers bit for bit ([`compare/RESULTS.md`](https://github.com/pmndrs/bvh/blob/main/compare/RESULTS.md), "compare/queries").
- **three-mesh-bvh, CPU:** refits Sponza (5.7M triangles) 1.3–1.5× faster. It was 6–7× faster until our refit stopped sorting and allocating per node, and 1.6–2× until the CPU query pass made it one pass. The knot and the room are now level (0.93–1.06×). We still write 48-byte triangle records and 3 million 64-byte nodes, where three-mesh-bvh writes 1.7 million 32-byte nodes over the app's positions. Its box queries (1.2–1.4× ours before) and closest point are 0.23–0.54× ours now. With SAH its sphere queries are a little faster than ours on the knot (1.05–1.13×) and Sponza (0.95–1.24×).
- **three-mesh-bvh, GPU:** its closest-hit walk is 1.1–1.2× ours on the furnished room. Its any-hit from outside the room was 3.2× our default kernel's: our any-hit enters the farther child first (right for shadow rays from surfaces), and a few rays per SIMD group of 32 went deep into the far side's furniture. With `anyHitOrder: 'larger'` (enter the larger child first) ours is 1.73× theirs on those rays; it stays a setting because it costs 0.78× on merged surface rays on the M5 and 0.80–0.87× on the RTX 3060 ([trace design](https://github.com/pmndrs/bvh/blob/main/docs/internals/design/trace.md)), and merged trees are the default.
- **bvh.js** builds its scene tree about 3× faster.
- **Memory:** our CPU trees (`CpuMeshBvh`, what the suite measures) hold about 9–10× more bytes beyond the app's geometry than three-mesh-bvh, because our triangle records copy the corners. On the GPU the default scene now has quantized nodes: Sponza 355.5 MiB beyond the geometry against three-mesh-bvh's 51.2, about 7×. Indexed leaves take it to 225.5 ([memory page](/performance/memory)). Building one huge mesh with tinybvh grows its WebAssembly memory to about 230 bytes per triangle while the build runs (Sponza as one mesh: 1.3 GiB); the builder now drops that memory afterwards and the worker pool ends such a worker, so the build leaves 391 MiB behind instead of 1.7 GiB ([workers](/workers/workers)).

## The compare pages

The suite also runs live in the [examples site](/examples/examples) (`examples/compare/`): each page puts the same
geometry through both libraries, checks every answer against ours, shows the tables and the CSV rows, and has a "what
else you can do" box with one thing only our pipeline does. Smoke runs use small scenes and short timings; the numbers
for this page come from full runs on the RTX 3060 and the M5, which are still to be taken for the two newest pages.

| Page | What it compares | What else (live) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `compare/raycast` | CPU first and all hits per second, every library; a GPU batch against three-mesh-bvh/webgpu | the batch is a raw WebGPU pass three never sees |
| `compare/build` | build, refit, memory; main-thread stalls of builds on the main thread and in workers | a GPU H-PLOC build of the same mesh |
| `compare/tree-quality` | SAH cost, depth, boxes, leaves, bytes | three builders, one layout |
| `compare/queries` | box, sphere and closest point (three-mesh-bvh, Rapier) | the GPU builder's tree answers the same CPU queries |
| `compare/scene` | scene trees over thousands of moving objects (bvh.js, `ObjectBVH`) | the GPU top-level refit every frame |
| `compare/gpu` | GPU closest and any hit against three-mesh-bvh/webgpu, paired rounds | a GPU-built tree traced by the same kernel |
| `compare/sdf` | SDF generation on the GPU against three-mesh-bvh's `webgpu_sdfGeneration` | the scene's GPU-built tree straight into a three texture |
| `compare/skinned` | skinned meshes kept on their pose every frame: three-mesh-bvh's `SkinnedMeshBVH.refit()` on the CPU against our GPU pose pass and refit, CPU and GPU ms per frame | a plain WebGPU pass, never seen by three, traces the refit trees every frame |
| `compare/pathtracer` | the same small path tracer on three-mesh-bvh/webgpu's WGSL functions and on our walk: Mrays/s for the whole tracer, its closest-hit rays and its any-hit rays | the scene's GPU-built trees traced by the same kernel |

### Method: `compare/skinned`

Nine skinned characters (tubes on a chain of six bones, `?characters=`, `?rings=`). In each measured frame both
libraries see the same pose (the bones are set from the frame's time), in alternating order:

- **three-mesh-bvh** (its `skinnedMesh` example): one `SkinnedMeshBVH` per character and `refit()` each frame, which
  skins every vertex on the CPU (`SkinnedMesh.getVertexPosition`). CPU ms is the refits' wall time. Its trees stay on
  the CPU; to trace the pose on the GPU it re-packs them with `BVHComputeData.update()`, which skins every vertex
  again. That is a row of its own (the upload itself happens at the next compute and is not counted).
- **ours**: `SceneTreesObject3DSync` with the renderer poses each `SkinnedMesh` on the GPU (`BvhPosePass`) and
  `SceneTrees.setPose` refits its tree from that buffer, in the frame's encoder. CPU ms is the sync and the recording;
  GPU ms is the pose passes (three's compute timestamps) plus what `SceneTrees.update` records (its
  `timestampWrites`), or without `timestamp-query` the wall time to the queue being done (an upper bound).

Medians over the measured frames. Tree quality is reported beside the times: three-mesh-bvh's summed SAH score against
its fresh build (`getBVHExtremes`), and our refit and drift-rebuild counts. The check: on a frozen pose, a top-down grid
of rays through our GPU-refit trees agrees with three-mesh-bvh's refit trees (CPU raycast) on every ray away from a
silhouette.

### Method: `compare/pathtracer`

One kernel (`examples/three/pathtracer/kernel.ts`: jittered camera rays, PCG random numbers, diffuse bounces, one sun
shadow ray per hit, accumulation) over two walks: ours through `BvhTraceNodes` (`traceClosest`, `traceAny`, the
library's defaults: merged static meshes, quantized nodes), and three-mesh-bvh's `BVHComputeData` (a `MeshBVH` with SAH
per geometry) through `bvh_RaycastFirstHit` and `bvh_RaycastAnyHit` in a `wgslFn`. Both read one palette colour per
mesh, so the shading is the same. Measured in paired rounds (the two back to back, the order reversed every other
round, the ratio taken within each round):

- **path**: every ray of the full tracer, closest and any hit;
- **closest**: the tracer with its shadow rays compiled out (the same closest-hit rays, since the shadow ray draws no
  random number);
- **any**: the shadow rays alone, derived as their count over the time the full tracer takes beyond the closest-only
  one (noisier than the other two).

Rays are counted in the kernel (one atomic per pixel); time is three's compute timestamps, else the wall time of one
dispatch with the queue drained before and after. Each pixel's first hit is checked against ours; a pixel through a
silhouette may graze either way between two different triangle tests, so up to 0.1% of pixels may differ.

## three-mesh-bvh's examples, ported

These are three-mesh-bvh's own examples on our API (`@pmndrs/bvh/three`), not timed comparisons. Each page says where it
departs from the original, and its smoke run checks our answers (against three-mesh-bvh's where the example asks a
query both answer).

| Page | Shows | Departure from the original |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `three/shapecast` | `intersectsSphere`, `intersectsBox`, `intersectsGeometry` against a spinning knot | none |
| `three/distancecast` | `closestPointToGeometry` (tree against tree) and a marching-cubes volume of `closestPointToPoint` | three's seeded `SimplexNoise` |
| `three/characterMovement` | a capsule walked through a level baked by `StaticGeometryGenerator`, pushed out of what `shapecast` hands back | a level of three's primitives instead of a downloaded dungeon |
| `three/physics` | spheres pushed out of an environment's triangles (`shapecast`, nearest boxes first) | an environment of three's primitives instead of a downloaded jungle |
| `three/sculpt` | brushes that find vertices with `shapecast` (contained boxes whole), then a refit | our `refit()` is whole-tree; three-mesh-bvh refits only the visited nodes |
| `three/selection` | lasso and box selection narrowed by each box's screen hull | the knot stays indexed |
